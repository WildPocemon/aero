Common faults

What usually brings us out

If any of this sounds like your kitchen, it is almost certainly a known pattern rather than a mystery.

Refrigeration

01 Warm on one side
On dual refrigeration units the freezer runs on while the fresh food side drifts. Usually an evaporator fan, a defrost fault or a failing sensor rather than the sealed system.
02 Running constantly
Nine times in ten this is a condenser packed with dust, or a door gasket that no longer pulls a vacuum. Both are inexpensive; both wreck a compressor if left.
03 Ice, water and frost
Blocked drain lines, a failed ice maker module, or a water valve that has silted up. Frost inside the cabinet points at a seal or a defrost circuit.
04 Vacuum-sealed doors
A door that will not reopen for a minute is normal. A door that never seals, or swings out of alignment, is a hinge and gasket job.
05 Panel and display faults
Service codes, dead panels and units that will not hold a setting — the control board is the usual answer, but we test before we replace.

Cooking

01 Burner will not light
Clicking with no flame is normally a spark module, a fouled igniter or a burner cap sitting proud. Rarely the valve, though we check it.
02 Oven off temperature
Baking that browns unevenly or runs cold is a calibration, sensor or element question. We measure the actual cavity temperature rather than trusting the display.
03 Convection problems
Noisy or stalled convection motors, and elements that no longer cycle correctly, take the even heat out of a range that was bought for exactly that.
04 Griddle and charbroiler
Uneven griddle plates, thermostats out of range, and charbroiler burners that will not hold a low setting.
05 Doors, hinges and glass
Heavy oven doors drop over the years. Hinges, springs and the inner glass seal are all serviceable rather than terminal.

Maintenance

The visit that prevents the repair

Most of the expensive failures we attend were preventable, and nearly all of them announce themselves months in advance. Our maintenance visit is a scheduled hour that catches them while they are still cheap.

Manufacturers ask for condenser cleaning at least annually — more often in a home with pets or near the coast, where salt air is unkind to a coil. Almost nobody does it. It is the single most common reason a compressor fails early.

Annual programme, per appliance $249

Two scheduled visits a year, priority booking, and any repair discounted by fifteen percent. Single visits are $179.

  1. Condenser cleaned properlyGrille off, coil brushed and vacuumed back to bare fin — not a pass with a duster through the vent.
  2. Seals tested, not eyeballedEvery gasket checked for pull and closure, hinges adjusted, and doors aligned back to specification.
  3. Temperatures verified independentlyCabinet, freezer and wine zones measured with our own calibrated instruments against the unit's own display.
  4. Water and ice systems flushedFilters replaced, lines and drains cleared, ice production timed over a full cycle.
  5. Burners and ovens calibratedIgniters cleaned, flame pattern checked across every burner, oven cavity temperature measured and corrected.
  6. A written condition reportWhat is sound, what is wearing, and what we would budget for in the next two years — with no obligation attached.

Questions

Worth asking

Are you an authorized Sub-Zero or Wolf servicer?
No. <name> is an independent company with no affiliation to, authorization from, or endorsement by Sub-Zero Group, Inc. We specialise in servicing their equipment and fit genuine manufacturer parts, but we are not a factory service centre. If your appliance is still inside its manufacturer warranty, contact Sub-Zero Group directly first — work by an independent company may affect that cover, and we would rather tell you that than take the booking.
My unit is out of warranty. Is a repair still sensible?
Usually, and by a wide margin. These appliances are built for twenty years or more, and the parts that fail — condensers, gaskets, igniters, control boards, fans — are all serviceable. A repair that runs a few hundred dollars against a replacement that runs many thousands, plus cabinetry work to fit a new unit into an existing opening, is rarely a close call.
How often should the condenser really be cleaned?
At least once a year, and every six months if you have pets, live near the coast, or the unit sits in a busy kitchen. A blocked condenser makes the compressor work harder and hotter for years before it fails, and by then the repair is an order of magnitude more expensive than the cleaning would have been.
Do you carry parts on the van?
For the common failures on both marques, yes — gaskets, igniters, spark modules, fans, sensors, filters and the control boards that fail most often. Because we work on only two brands, our stock goes deep rather than wide. Anything rarer is typically here within two to four working days.
Will you take the unit out of the cabinetry?
Only when the work genuinely requires it, and with the care that installation deserves. Floors are protected, custom panels come off by hand, and the appliance goes back square to the run with the doors realigned. Most repairs are completed in place.
What does a visit cost?
$149 for the diagnostic, credited in full against the repair if you go ahead. After diagnosis you receive one flat price for parts and labour before any work begins. The annual maintenance programme is $249 per appliance for two visits, or $179 for a single visit.
