Method

Diagnosis before parts

Most failed HVAC repairs come from swapping the obvious component without asking why it failed. Our technicians work a fixed sequence on every call, and you get the readings in writing.

  1. Measure the whole system

    Static pressure, temperature split, amp draw and refrigerant state — before anything is opened up.

  2. Find the root cause

    A burned contactor is a symptom. We trace what killed it, so the same part doesn't fail again next summer.

  3. Quote it flat, in writing

    Repair options with prices attached, plus the honest replacement comparison when the system warrants it.

  4. Verify before we leave

    The system runs through a full cycle with us there, and the final readings go on your invoice.

FAQ

Technical questions, plain answers

My AC still cools, just badly. Is that worth a call?
Usually yes, and early. Weak cooling normally means low refrigerant from a leak, a dirty coil, or restricted airflow — all of which make the compressor work harder. Compressors are the expensive part, so catching the cause early is what keeps a $300 repair from becoming a $6,000 replacement.
Do you charge extra for nights and weekends?
No. Our rate is the same at 2pm on a Tuesday as it is at 2am on a Sunday. Emergency dispatch is about availability, not surcharges.
How do you size a replacement system?
With a Manual J load calculation based on your home's square footage, insulation, window area and orientation — not by copying whatever was there before. Oversized systems short-cycle, cool unevenly and fail early; it's the most common installation mistake we find.
Which refrigerants do you handle?
R-410A and the newer low-GWP A2L refrigerants including R-454B, plus legacy R-22 systems for leak repair and retrofit advice. All our technicians hold EPA Section 608 certification.
Is the Comfort Club worth it if my system is new?
For most manufacturers, documented annual maintenance is a condition of keeping the equipment warranty valid — so on a new system it's often protecting several thousand dollars of coverage for the price of a couple of coffees a month.
